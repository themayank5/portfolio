# SELECTED WORK

## 01 — Nimbus Design System

### Designing the system behind scalable products

**Product Designer · Enterprise SaaS · Design Systems · Accessibility**

As enterprise SaaS products grow, inconsistency accumulates through hundreds of small design decisions. Different product areas were using different patterns, teams were repeatedly solving the same problems, and there was no shared design language between design and engineering.

### The challenge

Create a scalable design system that could establish a consistent visual language, reduce repeated design decisions, support dense enterprise interfaces, and provide clear rules without restricting product flexibility.

### The reframe

Instead of asking:

> "How should this screen look?"

I reframed the problem as:

> "What rules should determine how future screens are designed?"

That shift—from designing individual screens to designing the rules screens follow—became the foundation of the system.

### My approach

**01 — Understand**

Identified recurring visual and structural decisions across the product.

**02 — Define**

Established principles for hierarchy, meaning, spacing, structure, shape, and depth.

**03 — Systemize**

Translated those decisions into reusable foundations and semantic rules.

**04 — Apply**

Tested the system against real product scenarios and realistic information density.

### System architecture

**Foundation**

Color · Typography · Spacing · Sizing · Radius · Grid · Elevation · Borders · Opacity · Breakpoints

↓

**Semantic**

Text · Surface · Border · Icon · Action · Feedback · Selection · Focus · Data Visualization

↓

**Component**

Button · Input · Checkbox · Radio · Badge · Tooltip · Tabs · Modal · Card · Search · Pagination

↓

**Product UI**

Navigation · Layouts · Workflows · States · Feedback · Responsive experiences

### Guiding principles

**Consistency**
Repeated decisions should produce predictable outcomes.

**Meaning**
Tokens communicate product intent rather than simply storing values.

**Flexibility**
The system should provide constraints without forcing every interface into the same composition.

**Scalability**
New product experiences should reuse the same foundations instead of recreating the visual language.

### Impact

* Established a consistent visual language across product experiences.
* Reduced repeated design decisions through reusable foundations and patterns.
* Created a shared design language between designers and developers.
* Built a scalable foundation for future product experiences.

### Key learning

A design system is a **decision system**, not just a token library. Tokens define boundaries; principles define how those boundaries should be used.

# 02 — Proptifi

## Designing an AI-powered property visualization experience

**Product Designer · UI/UX · AI Product · Responsive Web · Design System**

**Duration:** 4 months
**Platform:** Responsive web application

### About the product

Proptifi is an AI-powered SaaS platform that helps homeowners, interior designers, and real estate professionals visualize property transformations without hiring a designer or architect.

Users upload an image, choose an AI tool, configure their requirements, and receive visual results within minutes.

The platform includes:

**Fresh Look · Sky Color · Colors & Textures · Text-to-Image · Precision+**

### The problem

Traditional property visualization is expensive, slow, and difficult to iterate on.

The design challenge was:

> **How might we make AI-powered property visualization simple enough for non-technical users while building confidence and trust in AI-generated results?**

### User pain points

* Difficult to visualize renovation ideas before committing time or money.
* Uncertainty about which AI tool to use.
* Traditional rendering is expensive and slow.
* Limited transparency around AI-generated results can reduce trust.

### Business goals

* Increase product engagement.
* Reduce visualization time.
* Simplify AI image generation.
* Encourage repeat usage.
* Improve conversion on premium AI tools.

### Discovery

I combined stakeholder requirements with:

**Stakeholder Interviews · Competitive Analysis · Secondary Research · User Journey Mapping · Feature Benchmarking**

### Key insight

Users wanted:

* Fast results.
* Minimal technical knowledge.
* High-quality outputs.
* A simple upload experience.
* Multiple variations to compare.

This led to a core design principle:

> **Make AI powerful without making the workflow feel technical.**

### Competitive opportunity

I benchmarked Proptifi against:

REimagine Home · Interior AI · Virtual Staging AI · AIHomeDesign

The opportunity was to combine:

**Unified dashboard + guided AI workflow + multiple AI editing modes + property-focused experience**

within a single product.

### User journey

**Traditional**

Hire designer → Wait days → Receive mockup → Request changes → Wait again

**Proptifi**

Login → Upload → Choose AI tool → Generate → Compare → Download

### Core user flow

Login
↓
Dashboard
↓
Select AI Tool
↓
Upload Image
↓
Configure
↓
Generate
↓
Compare Results
↓
Download / History

### Key design decisions

#### 01 — Upload-first onboarding

The upload action is familiar and immediately communicates how to begin.

**Why:**
Users could enter the product and start creating without first understanding complex AI configuration.

#### 02 — Consistent AI workflow

Each AI tool follows the same fundamental interaction model.

**Upload → Configure → Generate → Compare**

**Why:**
Learning one workflow makes the other AI tools easier to understand.

#### 03 — Before & after comparison

AI results can feel uncertain without a clear reference.

**Why:**
Side-by-side comparison makes the transformation immediately visible and helps users evaluate the generated result.

#### 04 — Persistent navigation

Users frequently switch between AI tools.

**Why:**
Persistent navigation supports exploration without requiring users to repeatedly return to a central screen.

#### 05 — Progressive complexity

The core workflow remains simple while configuration options are introduced when needed.

**Why:**
Non-technical users can complete the basic task without being overwhelmed by advanced controls.

### Three-step experience

**01 — Upload**

Drag-and-drop or browse with guidance on image quality.

**02 — Customize**

Configure property type, room type, viewing angle, design style, image quality, gallery preferences, and variations.

**03 — Explore & Compare**

Review generated variations, compare results, download favorites, remove unwanted results, or generate more.

### Design system

Built a semantic, context-scoped typography and UI system so designers could use named styles and reusable patterns instead of repeatedly choosing raw values.

The system supported:

* Semantic typography
* Color roles
* Spacing
* Radius
* Components
* Interaction states
* Responsive layouts
* Accessible color usage

### Outcome

A guided, trust-building workflow transformed a traditionally expensive and time-consuming visualization process into a self-serve digital experience designed to produce results within minutes.

The same interaction model also allowed five different AI tools to feel like one cohesive product.

# 03 — AI Command Center

## Building an accessible foundation for an AI dashboard

**UX Designer · Enterprise SaaS · Accessibility · Design System**

### The problem

The dashboard had accumulated UX debt through rapid product development:

* Unclear header hierarchy.
* Inconsistent CTA treatment.
* Inconsistent use of color and typography.
* Accessibility issues.
* No single documented source of truth for foundational design decisions.

Before redesigning the interface, the product needed a reliable foundation.

### Audit focus

I focused on three areas:

**01 — Header hierarchy**

Improved grouping and visual prioritization.

**02 — CTA hierarchy**

Created clearer action patterns across card states.

**03 — Accessibility**

Validated color combinations against WCAG requirements.

### Design foundation

#### Color

Created a three-layer color system:

**Brand**

Primary indigo · Secondary terracotta

**Neutral**

Text · Surfaces · Borders

**Semantic**

Info · Success · Warning · Danger · Progress · Disabled · Pending

Each semantic color was mapped to consistent roles:

**Text/Icon · Hover · Border · Light**

### Typography

Rather than relying on one generic scale, typography was scoped to actual product contexts:

Page Titles · Section Headers · Cards · Tables · Menus · KPIs · Modals · Notifications · Search · Dropdowns · System Messages

### Spacing

Established a 4px-based spacing scale with 9 steps.

### Radius

Established a 6-step radius scale plus a pill value for badges and chips.

### Accessibility validation

Ran contrast validation against **WCAG AA and AAA**.

Testing was performed based on actual UI usage:

* Brand colors as button backgrounds.
* Brand colors as badge backgrounds.
* White and dark text against colored surfaces.
* Grayscale text against white and light surfaces.
* Semantic colors across status components.

### System scale

**48** color tokens
**79** typography styles
**9** spacing steps
**7** radius tokens

### Outcome

Created a documented, accessibility-verified foundation that gave design and engineering a shared reference for color, typography, spacing, and component decisions.

Instead of relying on visual judgment for every screen, teams could work from a consistent and verifiable system.
